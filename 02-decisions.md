# 02 - Decisions

1. dayKey spine. Every loggable entity carries an indexed local-calendar dayKey string, frozen at log time. Reason: one unification contract across four domains is the core of the research design.
2. No cross-domain foreign keys. Reason: hard-wiring relationships between domains would pre-answer the research question. Relationships must be discoverable through the spine at read time.
3. Derived values are computed on demand, never stored. Reason: stored aggregates drift from source data and hide the unification mechanism being evaluated.
4. Data providers integrate through the DATA_SOURCE adapter seam. Reason: HealthKit must stay supplementary and swappable, not structural.
5. Records that can arrive from more than one source carry externalId and canonicalId. Reason: entity resolution needs provenance, and evaluation integrity needs superseded records kept.
6. Feature flags live in AppConfiguration, an @Observable class seeded from UserDefaults at init and written back on change, injected through the SwiftUI environment via the keyed entry. Views never read UserDefaults directly. Reason: flags are research variables and need one auditable seam, and a flag flip must re-render every consuming view immediately. Amended in phase 2A: the original @AppStorage struct could not drive live updates from inside an environment value.
7. Navigation is a six-tab bar in this order: Today, Diary, Workouts, Journal, Trends, Profile. Reason: each domain gets a stable, equal surface. Alternatives are in 03-rejected.
8. Strength training is canonical from first-party data. General activities are canonical from HealthKit when they conflict with a manual entry. Reason: the app is the richer source for lifting detail, sensors are the richer source for general movement.
9. Superseded manual entries are never deleted, only marked via canonicalId. Reason: provenance and evaluation integrity.
10. Local-only. No server, no accounts, no telemetry. Reason: privacy is part of what the study evaluates for acceptability, and the ethics route depends on health data never leaving the device. Amended in the pre-slice-05 audit (2026-07-28): the live-api arm permits outbound reference catalog queries (food and exercise databases), and the live-api branch stands in for main for the time being. Health records still never leave the device by sync or telemetry; catalog queries are the one outbound surface and the "what leaves this device" disclosure describes them. CLAUDE.md amended together with this entry.
11. Backend first, then frontend. The data layer is built and test-proven before feature UI begins. Reason: the prototype already validated the screens, so the rebuild's risk lives in the data layer.
12. The six-tab bar is a custom SwiftUI bar, not the native TabView item bar. Reason: iPhone's native tab bar shows at most five items and folds the rest into an automatic More screen, which would break decision 7.
13. The custom tab bar keeps all six screens alive in a ZStack. Only the selected screen is visible, hittable, and exposed to VoiceOver. Reason: navigation depth, scroll position, and half-typed forms must survive tab switches, matching native TabView keep-alive behaviour. Two known consequences I accept: onAppear fires once per screen at launch, so any future per-visit behaviour must key off the tab selection instead; a focused text field on a hidden tab would keep the keyboard up across a switch, to be handled when forms exist.
14. DayKey and WeekKey are pure component math on a caller-supplied calendar, with no DateFormatter anywhere. WeekKey takes an explicit timezone parameter and caches one ISO 8601 calendar statically. Malformed keys return nil or an empty array, never a crash, and parsing is strict: Calendar.date(from:) normalizes overflow, so keys are verified by round trip. Reason: the derived layer calls these in loops and needs thread-safe, allocation-light functions; fixed-timezone tests need the calendar and timezone knobs; a cached formatter cannot honour a per-call calendar.
15. Canonical storage is metric (heightCm, goalWeightKg, waterTargetMl); display converts. Height and weight units are independent toggles (usesMetricHeight, usesMetricWeight), not one switch. Reason: mixed units are the common UK case (feet and inches height with kg weight, or stone weight); splitting later would cost a schema version and migration, splitting now is free.
16. Every schema change is a new VersionedSchema with a migration stage, even purely additive ones. SchemaV1 contains UserProfile only; PellMigrationPlan carries V1 as its base with no stages until V2. Reason: retrofitting migration discipline after real user data exists is where data loss comes from.
17. The store is a named file at an explicit URL (Application Support/Pell/Pell.store). Before every on-disk open, the SQLite file set (store, -wal, -shm) is copied into a timestamped folder under Backups/, newest 3 kept, older pruned. The whole set is copied because WAL mode keeps recent transactions in the sidecars. Backups are best effort and never block launch. The launcher returns success or failure, never throws out of the app; the in-memory path for tests and previews skips disk and backups entirely. Reason: backups and exports need a findable file, pre-open copies are the cheapest insurance against migration accidents, and a cosmetic backup failure must not brick a launch.
18. PellStore (@MainActor @Observable) is the single write path: every mutation goes through it, save is explicit, and errors surface into lastError instead of crashing. profile() is fetch-or-create with a one-row invariant the schema cannot express; duplicates are returned first-wins and never deleted (dedup is the import slice's problem). PellStore is injected type-based with no fallback default: a view reading it without injection crashes in development. Reason: one auditable mutation seam; and a silent fallback store would swallow writes into a throwaway container, which is quiet data loss, while the loud crash is a two-minute development fix. On store failure the app shows RecoveryView (neutral copy, error text, retry, nothing destructive) instead of the tab shell.
19. Loggable entities compute dayKey in init from their date and a caller-supplied calendar, never later. Reason: decision 1 made executable at the model layer, no caller can create a record whose key disagrees with its date.
20. Provenance and resolution fields (localId, source, externalId, canonicalId, suppressed) ship on importable entities from day one, unused until slice 08. Reason: adding them later costs a schema version and a migration, adding them now is free. Same reasoning as decision 15.
21. One JournalEntry per dayKey, repeat check-ins update the same row. The check-in sheet and the journal screen write through the same operation. Reason: the calendar dots, day story, and correlations all read one mood per day, and the sheet is just a shortcut past the journal submenu.
22. Water subtract removes the most recent entry on that day. Reason: totals stay a plain sum and exports carry no negative rows.
23. Range constraints the schema cannot express (mood, energy, stress 1 to 5) are clamped at the store boundary. Reason: the store is the single gate every write passes, and the UI does not exist yet.
24. Developer tooling lives in DevTools/, every file wrapped whole in #if DEBUG, enforced by a guard test and a Release build check. Reason: the no-tooling-in-Release guarantee stays a checkable fact, not a habit.
25. The standard fixture is offset-addressed JSON embedded as a string constant in a DEBUG-only file, seeded through PellStore operations. A bundled .json resource was rejected: it ships in the Release bundle unless excluded by build phase tricks the file-sync project layout makes fragile. Reason: data stays recent forever, seeded rows pass the same path and clamps as logged rows, and Release carries nothing.
26. wipeAll() lives on PellStore, not behind DEBUG. Reason: it is a mutation, so it belongs on the write path, and the privacy center master erase reuses it later.
27. -seed-fixture only seeds an empty store; -reset-store deletes the store file set before open, leaving backups in place. Reason: arguments left on across relaunches must not duplicate rows, and combined they give a deterministic boot.
28. The developer console reads raw ModelContext, mutates only through PellStore. Reason: an inspector must show stored truth, not what store logic returns, and the write path stays single.
29. Strength sessions and general activities are separate entities: WorkoutSession (with sets) and ActivityEntry. Reason: their reconciliation winners differ under the source-of-truth rules, so the winner rule becomes a property of the type instead of shape inference on a merged entity. Mixed session lists are a UI concern, not a schema one. Decided in the pre-slice-05 audit.
30. WorkoutSession.endedAt, nil while a session is in progress. Reason: live logging is minimizable and resumable, so finished state must be explicit; it cannot be inferred from sets because a finished session can hold uncompleted sets. date is the start; minutes is stamped at finish and stays editable for quick-logged sessions.
31. ExerciseSet keeps its own date and dayKey. Sets hang off the session through a cascade delete relationship plus sessionLocalId for export fidelity. A session date edit recomputes dayKey on the session and all its sets in one store operation. Reason: per-exercise history, last-time baselines, and PRs read sets directly without joining sessions, and the keys can never disagree with the session.
32. Manual sleep and weight are one row per dayKey, fetch or create, repeat logs update the row, mirroring the decision 21 check-in pattern. Imported rows stay separate and reconcile under the source-of-truth rules. Reason: a second pass through the check-in sheet must edit, not duplicate.
33. distanceKm? on ActivityEntry and note? on WeightEntry ship now, inert. Reason: same as decisions 15 and 20, adding a field later costs a schema version and a migration, adding it now is free. Distance lives on ActivityEntry, not WorkoutSession, because only general activities carry distance.
34. At most one WorkoutSession is open at a time; startSession returns the existing open row when one exists. Reason: live logging resumes exactly one surface, and activeSession() must be unambiguous for the day card and relaunch resume.
35. exerciseId is a stable lowercase slug owned by the app; external catalogs map their ids onto ours, never the reverse. activityType is likewise a plain string key, not an enum. Reason: sets freeze exerciseId forever, so history must not be coupled to a provider's id space, and adding an activity or exercise must never cost a schema migration.
36. FoodEntry.catalogId is the namespaced reference to a food catalog item, separate from externalId, which stays reserved for user-data import identity. gramsLogged and servingText ride beside it, preserving the logged quantity, because catalog nutrient data is per 100 g and computed totals alone cannot be re-edited by quantity. Reason: a catalog-sourced log is still a manual entry, so the two provenance meanings must not share a field; adding the fields now rides the V3 rev for free, same reasoning as decisions 15, 20, and 33.
37. Derived services are pure functions over caller-fetched rows; nothing in Derived/ touches ModelContext or FetchDescriptor, enforced by the write guard, and the store grew read-only range fetches to feed them. Reason: dependencies point downward, every service tests with plain arrays, and the benchmark measures honest work.
38. The locked correlation set is seven pairs run by one median-split engine over a metric registry: ties to the low group, binary metrics split by value, minimum 5 days per group, plain group means only, no coefficient or verdict anywhere in the output shape. Reason: the registry makes fixed pairs, the pair explorer, and future metrics one mechanism, and the output is structurally incapable of inference, which is the recorded mitigation of the median-split critique. Amended CLAUDE.md's "three locked correlations" to seven, edited together with this entry.
39. A streak-qualifying day has at least two of the four core domains present; weight rows never count; skip days neither break nor extend, but a day that both qualifies and is skipped still extends, because logging always wins; a pending today never breaks, so a run ending yesterday is still current. Reason: my call for breadth over single-domain check-ins, with per-domain streaks preserving the single-domain signal, consistency-only progression never punishing a log, and slice 07 context tags plugging into the skip-day input.
40. Warmup sets count in set counts but never in volume load or 1RM; in-progress sessions are invisible to summaries, streaks, and correlations until finished. Reason: derived numbers must not shift under the user mid-workout, and volume convention excludes warmups.
41. The 90-day derived sweep (summaries, streaks, all locked correlations, fetches included) must complete under 500 ms, enforced by a benchmark test on a deterministic dense dataset. Reason: the architecture's "feels instant" budget as a checkable fact.
42. Formula parameters follow the 2026-07-29 metrics review: Brzycki capped at 10 reps, FAO/WHO/UNU-anchored activity multipliers with a 1.40 sedentary floor, goal adjustments framed as non-linear starting heuristics, the correlation engine framed as descriptive summarisation and never inference, every formula recorded with evidence and caveats in docs/references/formulas.md. Reason: the formulas must survive examiner scrutiny, and the two changed parameters were the only ones with citable evidence against them.
43. Custom food reuse needs no entity: history search, recents, quick-add chips, presets, and catalogId cover every reuse path. Reason: a library would be a third food source with its own surface for marginal gain.
44. Nine built-in context kinds ship with stable keys (rest-day, not-tracking, travel, sick, holiday, injury, exam, fasting, social), seeded idempotently at launch and after persona seeding, deactivatable, never deletable; custom kinds get full CRUD with generated keys, deleting one removes its tags; any tagged day is a streak skip day. Reason: rest-day and not-tracking have hard-coded consumers, the rest are plausible life context, and CLAUDE.md's skip wording reads as any tagged day.
45. The one-per-period upsert family extends: HabitLog one row per habit per day, ContextTag one row per kind per day, WeekPlan one row per week, WeeklyPulse one row per week clamped 1 to 10. Habit insights count "didn't" as active days since the habit's creation without a done row. A day counts as logged (hasAnyLog, the log-daily challenge) with any row including weight and habit toggles; tags alone never count, because a tag is context about a day, not a log. Reason: repeat passes must edit, never duplicate, the denominator must not starve or poison the comparison, and logging always wins while forgiveness never masquerades as logging.
46. Habit insights run configurable pairs through the correlation engine (any registry metric, either lag), shipping the four scope comparisons as defaults with the shared floor of 5. Reason: the registry design makes configurability free, and one engine keeps every insight surface under the same descriptive constraints.
47. Confirming a planned meal writes the FoodEntry and stores confirmedEntryId; deleting that entry releases the slot back to planned through a hook in deleteFood. Reason: deleting a winner releases its claim, and the planner must stay truthful after diary edits.
48. WeekPlan pins a week; WorkoutPlan.isActive is the standing default; exactly one plan is active, enforced at the store; deleting a plan clears its week assignments; adherence reads pinned-else-active. Reason: one ongoing plan with occasional pinned weeks matches training practice and keeps adherence unambiguous.
49. Per-domain erase ships as wipe(domain:) beside wipeAll with a fixed mapping: nutrition (food, water, presets, items, planned meals), training (sessions, sets, activities, plans, days, exercises, week plans), sleep, journal (check-ins, habits, logs, tags, custom kinds, events, pulses), body (weight). Built-in kinds survive journal erase; the profile is master-erase only. Fixture personas load through one seeding seam, the sparse logger beside the standard, and built-in kinds count as furniture, not user rows, in the empty-store rule. Reason: the privacy TODO discharges while the entity list is open, and evaluation seeding is data, not code.
50. The adapter seam is a capability-declaring DataSourceAdapter protocol with typed DTO batches, behind a static AdapterRegistry keyed by stable strings; UserProfile stores a per-domain source key and unknown keys fall back to manual. Reason: capabilities keep the fake honest and give the source pickers something real to interrogate, and the registry makes the protocol call and the settings call compose without hard-coding.
51. HealthKit is the integration hub: external apps reach Pell only by writing to Apple Health, the originating app is recorded in sourceDetail (display name and bundle id) at import time because sourceRevision is unreadable once a sample is gone, and any direct API adapter is a recorded privacy amendment before it is a spec discussion. Reason: every major fitness app already writes to HealthKit, so the hub gets their data with zero accounts, zero tokens, and zero new outbound surface; one conformance proves the seam without speculative scaffolding.
52. Sync shape: 365-day backfill in month chunks newest first with a persisted SyncState checkpoint (resume proven, complete re-runs are no-ops), 14-day refresh on debounced foreground (60 s, manual bypasses), no background delivery. Imports pass a quarantine gate (sleep capped 24 h, activities 48 h since ultra events are real, future skew 5 minutes per the Kerberos 300-second convention, structural bounds) and quarantined records are counted with reasons, never written, never silent.  Reason: resumability and determinism over speed, and no data loss is ever invisible.
53. Reconciliation runs as an idempotent post-import pass per touched dayKey: a pure function computes the desired end state and the store applies the diff, so a released loser is exactly a row absent from that state. Matching is temporal IoU at 0.5, one-to-one greedy, compatible types only; unmapped types never auto-match; below-threshold overlaps stay live and count ambiguous. canonicalId holds the winner's localId. Import-versus-import clusters resolve richer data, then earlier start, then lexicographic externalId. Sleep and weight resolve by day: manual wins, import-only days keep the longest night and the newest reading. Reason: the overlap coefficient scores containment 1.0 and guarantees the false merge it was meant to prevent; a false split is visible and fixable while a false merge hides data; the threshold is defended by the recorded sensitivity run.
54. Imported sleep assembles adapter-side: periods split at 1 hour of wakefulness (the published convention; 4 hours merged evening naps), the longest period per wake day is the night, shorter periods count as skippedAsNap and are not stored, timezone identifiers store beside the timestamps, inBed-only periods quarantine. Efficiency is never stored: SleepMath computes maintenance efficiency and time-in-bed efficiency as two named formulas, because one field must not silently carry two denominators. Stage minutes display but never enter the metric registry (low-validity signal). Reason: the gap is defended by the recorded sensitivity run, and computable values obey the derived rule.
55. Device energy is device-reported, not measured: Apple's basal figure is computed from profile inputs like our own formula arm and sits above a textbook BMR. EnergyMath uses the EnergyDay row when present else the estimate, DaySummary carries burnedSource, the chart layer renders a series break at a source change, and no delta or trend is ever computed across a source boundary. Reason: the two arms are different scales, so an unmarked switch renders as a fake behaviour change; naming the source is description, claiming measurement is not.
56. MET estimates: kcal = MET x kg x hours from the latest weigh-in on or before the activity date; no weigh-in means no estimate, matching the targets rule. Bands derive from each activity's Compendium code set with the code list and edition recorded per row; single-code activities show a point. The band claims intensity ambiguity only, never individual accuracy. Reason: every number stays traceable to a Compendium code for the write-up, and the Kozey critique is answered by editability plus the population-scope explainer.
57. The echo guard is two layers (own bundle id filter, hkMirrorId UUID backstop) and deletion is mirrored honestly: a deleted source record first releases its claims through the desired-state diff, then the imported row is removed, by explicit deleted list (reaching outside the refresh window) or by window set difference after a succeeded fetch. Manual rows are never deleted; deleteActivity and deleteSession reconcile their day so user deletes release claims too. Reason: one guard layer fails on rewritten source attribution; imported rows are mirrors and keeping a deleted mirror falsifies the store, while user-authored rows keep the decision 9 guarantee.
58. The outbox drains on foreground gated per row by nextAttemptAt with exponential backoff, attempts capped at 5, lastError retained after the cap, no jitter because there is one client and no shared server. Workouts and weight are the proving domains behind default-off toggles; the enqueue guard reads the profile without creating it; success stamps hkMirrorId. Reason: two write shapes exercise the whole pattern, and the jitter omission is a stated choice, not cargo-culting the pattern in reverse.
59. SyncLog records every run (trigger, duration, per-domain counts including ambiguous, quarantined with reasons, skippedAsNap, skippedAsEcho), rotated newest 50 runs or 90 days whichever keeps more. Coverage and co-coverage are derived functions, never stored; the reconciliation engine is evaluated in Ward's event taxonomy over a labelled corpus, with the tIoU and sleep-gap sensitivity tables recorded in docs/references/reconciliation-eval.md. Reason: the merge disclosure and privacy center need data that exists, co-coverage is the correlation engine's true denominator and the central statistic for the unification claim, and the hardest constants are defended by method, not assertion.
60. The export codec is one versioned JSON document: a header with formatVersion, schemaVersion, appVersion, exportedAt, and per-entity counts, one array per entity, hand-written Codable DTOs, deterministic encoding (ISO 8601 UTC with milliseconds, sorted keys, recorded per-entity sorts keyed on the encoded date string so sub-millisecond ties order identically before and after truncation), pure functions in Export/ under the write guard with an injectable now. A guard test over the live schema's attributes fails when a stored property has no DTO coding key, and a second guard fails when a schema entity has no export decision at all. Reason: inspectable by hand, provable byte for byte, and a new field or entity cannot silently escape the codec.
61. Export content policy: twenty-four entities travel; SyncState and PendingHKWrite never travel; SyncLog exports as a diagnostics section that import reads into the report and discards. Imported rows and all provenance fields travel verbatim with no dayKey recompute and no re-reconciliation, so a reconnect after restore upserts on externalId instead of duplicating. The profile travels with its HealthKit coupling reset at validation (source keys to manual, hkConnectedAt nil, write toggles off, flagged in the report). AppConfiguration flags stay out of the codec. Reason: device state restored is a lie, re-derivation would rewrite the merge history, sync history is evaluation evidence worth exporting and never worth restoring, and research arm assignment must not travel inside a data file.
62. Import versioning is forward-only: formatVersion 1, newer files refused with a clear message, older files pass an ordered transform list proven by a test-only transform. formatVersion versions the file contract; schemaVersion only records what wrote the file. Reason: one arm ships now, the seam is real rather than speculative, and future format changes never break old files.
63. Restore is replace only and atomic: strict rejection on structure, version, and duplicate identities (repair cannot reason about a corrupt identity space); counted repair on referential and range problems (orphans dropped, dangling canonicalId and confirmedEntryId released with a confirmed slot returning to planned, one-per-period duplicates dropped first-wins with imported rows exempt, the store-boundary clamps applied), every count surfaced in the import report, nothing silent. Order: validate, back up the file set through the pre-open machinery, then wipe and insert with one save on a throwaway scratch context, so failure is atomic by abandonment and never depends on rollback behaviour (SwiftData rollback() was observed keeping pending changes alive under load). Merge restore is rejected. Reason: replace is provable, a failed restore must leave the old store intact, and the quarantine gate rule applies to file data too.
64. PellStore gains the batch restore entry point: decoded rows insert with one save at the end, inside the single write path, relationships rebuilt from the flat id seams, built-in kinds upserted by key, system rows left empty. This is the recorded retirement path for the slice 08 per-row import save cost; rewiring the sync engine onto it is deferred to a later slice. Reason: restore is the one operation that legitimately writes thousands of rows at once, and the write path stays single.
65. Backups are plain JSON, named as unencrypted in the privacy disclosure. Reason: local-only means no key recovery, so a passphrase adds a way to lose data without adding a protection the threat model needs.
66. RecoveryView offers retry and start fresh; start fresh moves the unopenable file set whole into a timestamped SetAside folder behind an explicit confirmation, keeps every backup, deletes nothing; there is no export action on recovery because the codec cannot read a store that will not open. Reason: the button must not promise what the code cannot do, and the never-auto-wipe rule stands.
67. Frontend architecture: views read PellStore and AppConfiguration from the environment directly, there is no view model layer, and anything computed or testable lives in Derived/ or a pure helper under Features/Shared. Reason: the store is already the app's API with a single write path; a view model layer would mirror it one to one, and the write guard polices the seam that matters.
68. Features/Shared exists and grows only on demand: a component enters it when a surface needs it or a second tab asks for one Journal already built, never ahead of use. Seeded in slice 10 with RecallRule, ScoreRow, DayNavigator and DayLabel, CalendarGrid, MoodScale, and the explainer system. Reason: the seams that survived the backend phase were the ones proven by use; speculative shared UI is how design systems rot.
69. The check-in recall rule: before noon the sheet addresses yesterday, from noon today, hard-coded, and the sheet always names the day it writes and lets the user change it. Reason: honest recall is the point of the default, and naming the day makes the default visible instead of magical.
70. The metric explainer system: a reusable sheet over a registry of entries written against the real formulas, and every computed number rendered anywhere in the app carries an info affordance opening its entry. Established in Journal with five entries, inherited by every later tab. Reason: the locked cross-cutting rule made this a promise; building it with the first computed surfaces makes it structural instead of retrofitted.
71. The mood scale renders as a single-hue lightness ramp (hue 0.61, saturation 0.55, brightness 0.92 to 0.32 in five recorded steps, deeper is higher), identical by role in light and dark, with the mapping named in an explainer legend. Reason: a red-to-green ramp encodes judgment, which the tone rules forbid, and fails colour-blind users; lightness is ordinal without being evaluative.
72. The frontend test gate: interaction logic lives in pure helpers with unit tests, views stay thin and unproven by automation, each phase verifies its surfaces live in the simulator, and every slice wrap runs a recorded copy audit against the tone rules. No snapshot dependency. Reason: the load-bearing risks in this codebase are formulas and copy, not pixel drift, and the no-dependency rule stands.
73. The coach is a three-state setting (off, reflective, guiding), default off, gated by an in-app declaration accepted per tier step, and tier 2 is the only place in the app where evaluative or suggestive copy is permitted; CLAUDE.md's tone rule is narrowed to exactly that surface and no other. Acceptance never travels in the export. No ethics amendment required, confirmed 2026-07-30. Reason: the research question is about acceptability, a tier the user chooses behind explicit agreement is both the honest design and a usable comparison condition, and scoping the exception to one surface keeps every other screen governed by the original rule.
74. The coach never infers. Where a reason matters it asks a fixed-answer question and writes the answer as a ContextTag where a built-in kind exists (rest-day, travel, sick, injury), snapshot-only otherwise. Reason: the no-auto-inference rule points at the better design anyway, and an elicited answer is data the app owns rather than a guess it defends.
75. Every pattern message reports effect size, dispersion, day counts, and a seeded shuffle check shown in plain counts, and null results are surfaced as ordinary results. Reason: two bare means invite overreading, a coach that only reports hits manufactures patterns, and the honest sentence is the shown one.
76. All coach comparisons are against the user's own rolling history. No population norms or normative bands anywhere. Reason: the no social comparison rule, and personal baselines are the only reference a local-only single-user app can honestly claim.
77. Goals are authored in Profile, progressed on Today, and read-only to the coach; they name a metric key, never a domain row, and their progress is derived, never stored. Reason: goals must survive the coach being off, and the no cross-domain foreign key rule and the derived-not-stored rule both apply unchanged.
78. Goals are process goals over things the user controls, with a named exception for estimated 1RM targets. Reason: outcome goals were already rejected for badges for the same reason, and consistency of that rule matters more than convenience.
79. Coach restraint is engine-enforced, not guidance: a suggestion ignored twice retires permanently, a category dismissed twice mutes for 30 days, a weekly budget caps volume, and suppressors silence rules during declared disruption. Reason: escalation is the failure mode of every coaching feature, and a rule with a test is the only version that holds.
80. Every coach message carries an explainer chip, a "why did I see this" panel naming its rule and inputs, and a feedback control with reason codes, and the whole message log persists and exports. Reason: auditability is what separates this from a black box, and the log is the evaluation instrument.
81. The coach speaks only inside the app. No notifications, and no coach content in Trends or the monthly report. Reason: push framing converts reflection into pressure, and mixing advisory content into the report would make the descriptive surfaces advisory by association.
82. Coach copy lives in templates with named slots, the copy audit runs over the template set, and the coach entities ride the export at formatVersion 2 through the slice 09 transform seam. Reason: a finite enumerable string set is provable, and a file whose contract grew must refuse old readers rather than silently dropping data.
83. SchemaV6 is additive and lightweight: dietary and water source keys on UserProfile completing the decision 50 pattern, with the riders that were waiting for the next real rev (SyncState.lastAutoExportAt and lastRestoreAt from the slice 09 deferral, ContextKindDefinition.colorKey). Export stays at formatVersion 1: no entity was added, the new source keys join the HealthKit coupling reset, colorKey defaults harmlessly, and the two new DTO fields decode leniently so pre-V6 files restore with defaults. Found at the container: the staged migration plan crashes Core Data with "Duplicate version checksums detected" at six versions because every versioned schema shares the same live model classes; the container now opens with automatic lightweight migration, the declared chain remains the version paper trail, and a future non-additive change must bring per-version model snapshots. Reason: the fields are needed, the riders ride per the 33 and 36 precedent, and the honest mechanism is the one that works rather than the one that reads well.
84. The food catalog is a seam with two providers behind separate toggles: a bundled catalog of 352 items derived-in-style from USDA public-domain per-100g data as embedded JSON with bundled: ids (values approximate, regenerable straight from FDC as a recorded data task), and the USDA FoodData Central online arm. A catalog hit logs as a manual FoodEntry with catalogId, gramsLogged, and servingText, decision 36 unchanged. Reason: one seam keeps the add-food flow one screen, and a catalog-sourced log stays a manual entry.
85. The online arm fires only on explicit user search submits and caches nothing: results live in transient screen state and are discarded on dismiss, and nothing catalog-shaped persists beyond the entries the user logs. The no-network guard exists from this slice: every Swift file in the app target is scanned for networking symbols with exactly one allowlisted file (USDAFoodCatalog.swift), red-tested on a planted violation; the client tests run against a stubbed transport so the suite never touches the network. Reason: the amendment allows catalog queries, not a cache of external data on device, and the guard is exactly as wide as the amendment.
86. Imported dietary and water land as one aggregate row per day and domain on the existing entities with day-keyed externalIds (hk-dietary-<day>, hk-water-<day>), upserted, quarantined structurally with the 300-second future skew, never per-sample. Reason: per-sample rows would flood the diary with entries no user logged, the aggregate is what summaries and the coverage strip need, and the suppressed field has waited on both entities since slice 3 for exactly this.
87. The day-exclusive arm suppresses without zeroing and releases on the last manual delete: any manual food row suppresses that day's dietary aggregate, any manual water row suppresses that day's water aggregate, the two domains decide independently, and logFood, addWater, deleteFood, and subtractWater reconcile their day so removing the last manual row un-suppresses the import. subtractWater targets manual rows only, since a mirror is never the undo target. Reason: bedtime consistency was import only by convention, not by rule, and a typed bedtime is the same fact as an imported one.
229. The Journal month calendar merges into History as a card above the filter pills, wrapping UICalendarView with mood as the native decoration dot, selection cleared after firing, future days greyed; a day tap opens the day story and the standalone calendar screen is deleted. Reason: two calendars was one too many, and the native view carries the accessibility and paging a hand built grid fakes.
230. (ruled 2026-08-17) No counted mode capture path may write a food entry whose values are absent. A declared zero is a measurement and stays loggable: water, diet drinks and black coffee are real zeros. Simple diary mode remains the only numberless path. Reason: the custom food form already refused a zero calorie save in counted mode, and Describe a meal plus the barcode portion step were bypassing that rule, so the app had two answers to the same question. Absence and zero are different facts and the capture surfaces now say which one they hold.
231. The Open Food Facts parser reads the `_prepared_100g` keys when a row declares no energy as sold, and takes all seven nutrients from whichever basis wins, never a mix. Prepared values are labelled on the portion step and recorded in the entry's serving line as "as prepared". Reason: cordials, drink powders and cup soups declare only the made up figures, so the plain keys read zero out of a complete row. A prepared figure describes a different thing from the pack, so it is imported labelled or not at all.
232. A scanned barcode that resolves to a product with no energy on either basis gets its own state naming that the database holds no values, with one button into the custom food form prefilled with the scanned name. It is not a detected product worth zero. Reason: an unknown barcode 404s and reads honestly; a known barcode with no nutrition facts returned 200 and presented as a real food at zero calories. Open Food Facts holds 37,514 UK products in that state.
233. The meal text parser ranks logged history rows carrying no values below the bundled catalog: history with values first, then the catalog, then valueless history. Reason: history outranked the catalog unconditionally, so one name-only entry set that food to zero for every later parse, permanently and invisibly. Ranking below rather than excluding keeps a simple mode user's own logged names matchable.
234. Absent sodium falls back to salt divided by 2.5 in the Open Food Facts parser. Reason: the same fact stated the other way round, and rows carrying only salt were reading as zero sodium.
235. A catalog serving grams of nil or zero falls back to 100 g at every read, through `CatalogFood.unitGrams`. Reason: the portion step already guarded for it and the meal text sheet did not, so a zero serving would have scaled every nutrient to nothing.
236. The Open Food Facts parser reads the pack size (quantity, product_quantity, product_quantity_unit) beside the serving fields, and the pack can stand in for the serving. A declared serving larger than the pack is dropped as the per 100 g basis typed in as a serving. A pack of 150 g or less stands in for a missing serving, labelled "1 pack (...)". Multipack totals and share bags never stand in, and prepared rows are exempt from both rules. Reason: single packs were defaulting to 100 g, so a 25 g packet logged four times its calories, and the live database carries the per 100 g basis as the declared serving on many rows (the Mars bar row declares a 100 g serving on a 51 g pack). 150 g is the bound because single packs sit well under it, while a share bag above it should stay an explicit choice rather than a default.
